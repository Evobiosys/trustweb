On your phone

Most of it stays there

Your web of people, notes, resources, transcripts, recordings and settings are kept in the browser storage of your own phone. We do not receive them. Deleting them is your choice: "Reset this phone" in Settings removes everything TrustWeb stored on it.

Through our server

What passes through the server in Switzerland

Our server runs at Infomaniak, in Switzerland. Several things pass through it, each one only because you used the matching feature:

  • The relay. It carries messages between phones: asks, answers and the links to shared meetings. They are encrypted on one phone and opened on the other, so the relay cannot read them. It can see routing details (which address a message is for, from which network address it was sent, and when). It holds a message it could not deliver for 72 hours at most (older ones are cleared when the relay next handles a message), and drops it as soon as the receiving phone confirms it. The one exception to the encryption is the very first connection step between two phones, sent before they share a key: that message is not encrypted.
  • Notifications. Off unless you switch them on. When they are on and a message waits for your phone while the app is closed, the relay wakes the phone through the push service of your platform; the message itself stays on the relay until your phone reads it. In the Android app and in browsers, that wake is empty: the push service (Google's for Chrome, Mozilla's for Firefox) learns that something waits for your device, and when. On iPhone and iPad, Apple makes it mandatory that every notification passes through Apple's push service, and Apple allows no empty one. So the relay sends one with fixed words ("Something arrived for you") and nothing else in it; before it shows, TrustWeb's own notification extension on your phone reads the waiting message itself, under your keys, and replaces the fixed words with the real ones. Apple sees that something arrived for your device and when, never what, from whom, or of which kind. We cannot switch that off; if you would rather Apple saw nothing, leave notifications off on an Apple device and the app shows what arrived when you open it. For this, the relay keeps one address per phone (a push subscription or an Apple device token), replaced when the phone re-registers and removed when you switch notifications off.
  • Encrypted backup. A backup is encrypted on your phone before it is uploaded. The server keeps ciphertext it cannot read, with its size and when it was last written. There is no automatic expiry for a backup, and no delete button for it yet; the data policy says what you can do.
  • Sending a meeting. The notes and transcript are encrypted with a fresh key and stored on the same server. The key is only in the link you send, and that link reaches the other person as an encrypted message, so the server never holds the key.
  • The front doors. A "front door" is a small always-on TrustWeb contact that runs on this server, with no person at the keyboard. There are two, both run by the person named in the imprint: the demo front door, named "Jakob" (its name may change), which holds lines its owner chose to publish in advance and marks as demo content, and a personal one, which answers only from lines its owner allowed in advance. Both keep the name and the identifier of each phone that connected to them, in a file only the service can read. An ask sent to a front door is opened there and matched against its lines, and it is also passed on, with the name you use, to the phone of the person who runs it, who can answer it live or not at all. The front doors keep no copy of an ask on disk, and their logs keep counts and outcomes, never the text of an ask or a name.
  • Short connect links. Where the app offers a short link next to the long one, the long connect link (which carries the name you chose and what another phone needs to connect to yours) is stored on our server under a random ten-character id for up to 30 days, and the short link opens it. Our service does not write the link or its id to any log (if our server fails at the moment a short link is opened, the web server's error log could still name it). The long link is a capability you already share openly, for example in a QR code, and it is not encrypted on the server beyond the connection itself, so share a short link only where you would share the long one. Making one is limited per network address (counted in memory). You can always show and use the long link instead.
  • Hosted recordings (starting soon, experimental). When this is switched on, your recordings will be stored on the server as ciphertext it cannot read, with each one's size, length and when it was last written or opened, counted against your allowance. You can delete a recording from the app at any time. A recording that is neither written nor opened for 90 days may be removed; that clean-up is run by hand today, not on a timer.

  • Network addresses reach the server with every request. TrustWeb's own services (backup, hosting, transcription, feedback, web results, short links) count requests per network address in memory only, to limit abuse: nothing about the count is written to disk and it is gone when the service restarts. That is not anonymous: while it is held, it is tied to your address. The web server keeps no access log. Its error log, in the server's system journal, can note the network address and the page requested when a request fails (for example while a service restarts), never the content of a request. TrustWeb's own services write log lines that hold a method and a status, or an outcome, and no address. Two older services on the same server, the newsletter service and the ReBioSys relay that the main page's waitlist form used before 2026-10-07, do write the requester's network address in their system logs; see the data policy.

Web results

Using web results

TrustWeb can show a few web results inside the app, below the answer from your own web. They are outside TrustWeb and untrusted. Powered by Brave.

  • What you agree to. When you use "Show results here", you agree not to scrape, store, cache or redistribute the results. They come from Brave, and they are shown only to you, for the moment.
  • Your search words. The words you type into the web results go through our server to Brave Search, a Brave based search engine, without your network address. Brave may log them under its own terms.
  • What we keep. Our server keeps no copy of your words or of the results. To limit abuse it counts searches per network address, in memory only (never written to disk, gone when the server restarts, and not anonymous while held), and its log notes only the engine and a status.
  • The plain links to Google, Ecosia, Qwant and Brave work differently: your browser, not our server, sends the words, and only when you tap one.

This website

What it does when you visit

  • No analytics, no tracking cookies, no advertising, and nothing stored in your browser.
  • Typefaces load from Google Fonts. That means your browser contacts Google, and Google receives your network address.
  • Typefaces come from your own device. This site fetches no fonts, scripts or images from anyone else.
  • The web server is not configured to write access logs for these sites.
  • The "notify me" form on the main page sends your email address to our server, only when you press the button, and keeps it for one note when TrustWeb is stable. The optional newsletter box passes it on to our newsletter service (see below). It is stored like a feedback message (next point).
  • The feedback form sends what you type, the kind of message and the page you wrote from. It sends your email address only if you tick a box. All of it is stored on our server in Switzerland with the time to the minute, in a file only the server can read. We do not store your network address or browser details. We read the messages by hand.
  • The three boxes on the feedback form are all unticked, and your address is not sent unless you tick one. "Write back to me about this message": we keep your address so we can answer. "Notify me when it is ready": we keep it for one note when TrustWeb is ready. "Stay informed with our newsletter": we keep it, and our server passes it on to our newsletter service, which first sends you a confirmation email; you are on the list only after you click the link in it. You can ask us at any time to take you off, or use the unsubscribe link in any newsletter email, which deletes your address from that service. The newsletter service runs on our own server.

Why we may do this

Legal bases, in plain words

  • To give you what you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR): the relay, backup, hosted recordings, sending a meeting, the front doors and short links exist only because you use them.
  • Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)): audio leaving your phone for transcription, "Show results here", and every box you tick or form you send with your email address. You can withdraw it at any time by writing to us; that does not affect what was done before.
  • Our legitimate interest in keeping the service safe (Art. 6(1)(f)): the per-address counters that limit abuse, and the short-lived technical logs. You may object to this.
  • We do not use your data for advertising, profiling or analytics, and we do not sell it.

Your rights

Access, correction, deletion, complaint

You can ask us what we hold about you (access), to correct it, to delete it (erasure), to restrict its use, to receive it in a usable form (portability), and you can object to processing based on our legitimate interest and withdraw a consent. Write to connect@evobiosys.org; we answer within one month. Most of your data is only on your phone, where you control it. Ciphertext on our server (backups, hosted recordings, sent meetings) cannot be tied to you by us, because we hold no account, name or email address for it: to find yours we need an identifier that only your phone can derive. How that works is to confirm; the data policy says what is possible today.

You can also complain to the Austrian data protection authority, the Datenschutzbehörde.

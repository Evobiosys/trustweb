FAQ

The questions people ask first, answered as TrustWeb works today. Where the honest answer is "not yet", it says so.

Is this not just Facebook with a different look? (Ist das nicht Facebook in anderer Darstellung?)

Facebook lets you repost. TrustWeb lets you ask, and each person you trust decides whether to answer.

Reposting is a poor way to find anything: you hear about an offer only if you follow the right channel or someone happens to repost it. In TrustWeb you call into your web with a question in plain words, and the people you are connected with decide, one by one, whether to share. It is built so that, in time, a question can travel on through several steps of trust, with consent at each step, instead of being broadcast. Today a question reaches the people you are directly connected with; the demo walkthrough shows a scripted example of the next step.A question can travel on through friends instead of being broadcast: you choose how far (one hop by default). Everyone who answers says yes; a friend in the middle passes it on only if they switched that on. The rings on the screen are one of many ways to draw a network. What counts is what you can do on top of it. A feed, where you tell people things, is optional and early: it is off by default, and you switch it on in Settings.

Do I have to document everything I do and say, all day? (rund um die Uhr)

No. There are two ways in, and neither needs you to log your day.

One conversation at a time. Record a meeting, or speak a short debrief after an event ("I just met this person, they have this opportunity"). The app makes a transcript, and you pick the lines worth keeping as notes or as lines in My Resources. Each such line remembers the moment in the recording it came from. Trust grows one conversation at a time, and your resources grow the same way.

One set-up step. If you already keep notes elsewhere, an import can add them as lines in My Resources. Imported lines show as unreviewed until you keep them (one tap per line, or "Mark all reviewed" for a whole import); lines you recorded or typed show as created step by step. Imported lines start as "Only me": nobody can ask about them until you say so. Today the import file is prepared on a Mac from one notes tool, so this route is for early users.

Is it encrypted? How do I know my most sensitive data is safe? (Verschlüsselt? Sensible Daten?)

The problem exists without any app: what you tell people, they can pass on. What TrustWeb adds is control. You share only what you choose, and each line in My Resources has its own audience: acquaintances (everyone in your rings), friends, close friends, or only you.

Where things live: your notes, resources, transcripts and recordings stay on your phone. There is no central database of everyone's resources to attack. A question goes from phone to phone through a relay on our server in Switzerland. Messages are encrypted on one phone and opened on the other, so the relay cannot read them; it can see routing details, such as which address a message is for and when it arrived. The very first connection step between two phones, sent before they share a key, is the one message that is not encrypted. Backups, hosted recordings and sent meetings are encrypted on your phone first.

We do not claim a "zero-knowledge" system as a whole, because two things do read content. The demo front door ("Jakob" today, a demo contact on our server that holds the lines its owner chose to publish in advance, marked as demo content) opens an ask sent to it and also shows it, with your demo name, to the person who runs it. And server transcription, once it is switched on (it is off today), handles your audio as audio, because the audio itself goes to be turned into text. Details are on the privacy page.

How trusted is a "trusted network" really? (Wie vertrauenswürdig ist das Netzwerk?)

Trust in TrustWeb is not a score. You place each person in one of three rings you choose yourself (close friend, friend, acquaintance). Only people you have connected with can ask you anything, the ring sets what they may ask about, and every answer needs a yes from you: given live, or given in advance for a line you marked that way. A no looks exactly like "no match", so a decline gives nothing away. The honest limit: a network is only as trusted as the people you let into it, and TrustWeb cannot make a stranger trustworthy. Share only what you would be content to see passed on.

How do you make it a habit people keep? (Wie wird es zur Gewohnheit?)

By pull, not push. Many people switch push notifications off, so TrustWeb does not depend on them: asks that reach you wait in one place for you to review. The reason to open the app is that you are looking for something and want to find it. Onboarding and habit are the hard part, and they are early: today the demo is the way in, with imaginary friends and nothing to set up.

What is the one use case other apps do not already have? Why open TrustWeb instead of a dedicated app?

TrustWeb works like a neighbourhood, just online, much faster: you ask the people around you for what you need, and those who can help answer. For now the questions are about opportunities, resources and wants: what is on offer, what could be lent, what someone is looking for. Questions about knowledge come later.

Finding people and things through people you trust. You do not open TrustWeb to call a ride, a dedicated app does that better. You open it for what is not listed anywhere, or where it matters who is on the other end: who knows a good pediatrician, who has a room free for a few weeks, who has done this before. A marketplace could run on top of it later (marketplace is a planned use case, see use cases).

Where would web results come from? How can you trust a Google API?

You should not have to. TrustWeb searches your own web first and leaves the open web to you. Below the search box (once you have typed words) and below every finished ask, a row "Search the web instead" offers plain links to Google, Ecosia, Qwant and Brave, carrying the same words. The row is labelled as outside TrustWeb, with untrusted results; nothing is sent to any engine until you tap one, and it opens in a new tab with no referrer. Where we have switched it on, "Show results here" under the Brave row shows a few results inside the app instead: after you agree once, your words go through our server to Brave (a Brave based search engine), without your network address, and the results come back labelled as untrusted. The trusted answer comes first. The open web only comes second.

So it is first of all for meeting people, and for introductions? (Kennenlernen, Introductions?)

Yes, meeting people and introductions come first. An example: someone with many years of experience in a field sits in a contact's resources. You search for that topic, you see the line, and you ask for an introduction through the person you both trust. Shared interests are what connects people. How do you meet someone you have not connected with yet? (Kennenlernen offline?)

Meeting in person and sharing your contact on the spot is in the works.

One person shares their connect code. When you meet someone, they can share a QR code or a link; you scan it with your camera and the app opens with that person already selected. You choose your ring (close friend, friend, acquaintance) and press "Add to my web". If they share a Signal or WhatsApp link, one press opens your direct channel to them.

About the server. Our server keeps your connect code (the one the QR holds) and, if you want, a username you choose, so people can find you by name. That is the freemium offer. Your phone registers the code the first time you show your QR; without our server, people can still connect through the long link and your address (DID). The server does not know your contacts or your messages; it holds the connect code, the link it points to (your TrustWeb address and the name you chose), a fingerprint of a secret only your phone knows, and the username if you claimed one.

Or type instead of scanning. If you have their TrustWeb code, their name on TrustWeb, or their address (a DID: a unique identifier that works without a server), you can type it instead of scanning.

Advanced. You can choose to share only your TrustWeb code (the QR), only a Signal or WhatsApp link, or both.

A transcript read weeks later has lost the feeling of the conversation. How useful is it then? (Emotionale Verbindung?)

That is why the recording is kept next to the transcript, not only the text. Where the audio was kept on your phone, you tap a transcript line, or a line you noted, and the recording plays from that moment, so you can hear how it was said. Recording a meeting needs a tick that the other person agreed.

Is this safe? (Ist das sicher?)

TrustWeb is a web app. It runs in your browser or from your home screen, and you can remove it any time in Settings.

Your contacts and notes stay on this phone. Nothing about them leaves the phone without your yes.

Opening Meet registers a short address for your code on our server: your name, your username if you chose one, and a public key so other phones can reach you. When you connect through TrustWeb, your contact links go only to that person, encrypted.

The person who shared this code with you shared their name and the contact links they chose, for example Signal or WhatsApp. You can also use those links outside this app.

If you do not want TrustWeb, just leave it, or remove it in Settings. The Signal or WhatsApp link still works on its own.

You can read the open source code on GitHub.

What is trustweb.one/<name>?

Your address on TrustWeb. Anyone who opens it lands in TrustWeb, creates their own web in one step, and shows on your "intending to connect" ring as "reached out". You decide what happens next.

You claim the name in the app (Settings, then username). If this website ever has a page of that name, app.trustweb.one/<name> always works.

Only the connect itself reaches a server: the relay carries it, encrypted. Example: trustweb.one/japossert.

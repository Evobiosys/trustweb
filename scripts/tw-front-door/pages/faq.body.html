FAQ

The questions people ask first, answered as TrustWeb works today. Where the honest answer is "not yet", it says so.

Is this not just Facebook with a different look? (Ist das nicht Facebook in anderer Darstellung?)

Facebook lets you repost. TrustWeb lets you ask, and the people you trust decide whether to answer or pass it on.

Reposting is a poor way to find anything: you hear about an offer only if you follow the right channel or someone happens to repost it. In TrustWeb you ask your web a question in plain words, and the people you are connected with decide, one by one, whether to share. The aim is that a question can travel on through several steps of trust, with consent at each step, instead of being broadcast. Today a question reaches the people you are directly connected with; passing it on a further step is in the works. The rings on the screen are one of many ways to draw a network. What counts is what you can do on top of it. A feed, where you tell people things, is optional and comes later.

Do I have to document everything I do and say, all day? (rund um die Uhr)

No. There are two ways in, and neither needs you to log your day.

One conversation at a time. Record a meeting, or speak a short debrief after an event ("I just met this person, they have this opportunity"). The app makes a transcript, and you pick the lines worth keeping as notes or as lines of your inventory. Each such line remembers the moment in the recording it came from. Trust grows one conversation at a time, and your inventory grows the same way.

One set-up step. If you already keep notes elsewhere, an import can add them as lines of your inventory, each marked with where it came from, so you can tell imported lines from the ones you made step by step. Imported lines start as "Only me": nobody can ask about them until you say so. Today the import file is prepared on a Mac from one notes tool, so this route is for early users.

What makes people put their information in? (Was motiviert die Leute?)

You do not fill in a form for the network's sake. You note what you learned in a conversation anyway, and you get something back when you look for something: you search, and you find. The idea for a group is that someone well connected starts with a good inventory, so the first searches already find something. And you decide for each line who may ask about it, down to "Only me".

Is it encrypted? How do I know my most sensitive data is safe? (Verschlüsselt? Sensible Daten?)

The problem exists without any app: what you tell people, they can pass on. What TrustWeb adds is control. You share only what you choose, and each inventory line has its own audience: friends of friends, your friends, or only you.

Where things live: your notes, inventory, transcripts and recordings stay on your phone. There is no central database of everyone's inventory to attack. A question goes from phone to phone through a relay on our server in Switzerland. Messages are encrypted on one phone and opened on the other, so the relay cannot read them; it can see routing details, such as which address a message is for and when it arrived. The very first connection step between two phones, sent before they share a key, is the one message that is not encrypted. Backups, hosted recordings and sent meetings are encrypted on your phone first.

We do not claim a "zero-knowledge" system as a whole, because two things do read content. The demo inventory ("Demo inventory", a demo contact on our server with made-up lines) opens an ask sent to it and also shows it, with your demo name, to the person who runs it. And server transcription, once it is switched on (it is off today), handles your audio as audio, because the audio itself goes to be turned into text. Details are on the privacy page.

How trusted is a "trusted network" really? (Wie vertrauenswürdig ist das Netzwerk?)

Trust in TrustWeb is not a score. You place each person in one of three rings you choose yourself (close friend, friend, acquaintance). Only people you have connected with can ask you anything, the ring sets what they may ask about, and every answer needs a yes from you: given live, or given in advance for a line you marked that way. A no looks exactly like "no match", so a decline gives nothing away. The honest limit: a network is only as trusted as the people you let into it, and TrustWeb cannot make a stranger trustworthy. Share only what you would be content to see passed on.

How do you make it a habit people keep? (Wie wird es zur Gewohnheit?)

By pull, not push. Many people switch push notifications off, so TrustWeb does not depend on them: asks that reach you wait in one place for you to review. The reason to open the app is that you are looking for something and want to find it. Onboarding and habit are the hard part, and they are early: today the demo is the way in, with imaginary friends and nothing to set up.

What is the one use case other apps do not already have? Why open TrustWeb instead of a dedicated app?

Finding people and things through people you trust. You do not open TrustWeb to call a ride, a dedicated app does that better. You open it for what is not listed anywhere, or where it matters who is on the other end: who knows a good pediatrician, who has a room free for a few weeks, who has done this before. A marketplace could run on top of it later (marketplace is a planned use case, see use cases).

Where would web results come from? How can you trust a Google API?

You should not have to, and today TrustWeb searches your own web only: no web results, no Google. A fallback is planned and not in the app yet: when your web has no match, a button would run an ordinary web search at an engine you pick (for example Google, Ecosia, Qwant or Brave), with the results labelled as untrusted, and nothing would be sent to any engine before you tap. The trusted answer comes first. The open web only comes second.

As a user, do I really need this? I am worn out by apps. (Brauche ich das wirklich?)

You do not need the app. You need the things you can find through it (Du brauchst die App nicht, du brauchst die Sachen, die du durch sie findest.) TrustWeb is meant as one way in with several uses (meeting people, introductions, lending and sharing), not another feed to check. If asking a friend already gets you what you need, you do not need it. It is for the cases where you would ask a friend, but the friend does not know, and you do not want to ask three hundred people in a group chat. Try the demo: it needs no setup.

So it is first of all for meeting people, and for introductions? (Kennenlernen, Introductions?)

Yes, meeting people and introductions come first. An example: someone with many years of experience in a field sits in a contact's inventory. You search for that topic, you see the line, and you ask for an introduction through the person you both trust. Shared interests are what connects people. Today you ask the people you are directly connected with; passing a question on a further step, so an introduction stays warm, is in the works.

A transcript read weeks later has lost the feeling of the conversation. How useful is it then? (Emotionale Verbindung?)

That is why the recording is kept next to the transcript, not only the text. Where the audio was kept on your phone, you tap a transcript line, or a line you noted, and the recording plays from that moment, so you can hear how it was said. Recording a meeting needs a tick that the other person agreed.

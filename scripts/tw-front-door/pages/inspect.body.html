Inspect us: code you can read, servers you can visit, rights that travel with you

Why trust us, checked rather than promised

Code became cheap. Trust did not. So we compete on the three things you can check yourself.

Code transparency

Every line that runs TrustWeb is public

TrustWeb is published under the AGPL at github.com/Evobiosys/web-of-trust: the code on our servers is the code anyone can read, run and audit. Self-hosting is free, always. Enterprise and NGO licences exist for organisations that need them, and a licence for the commons (ALPS-inspired) is in preparation. A foundation will hold the code so that no future owner can close it.

Hosting you can visit

Pick the jurisdiction, then come and see the machines

Hosted TrustWeb runs on Swiss servers (ungleich) and on Austrian servers. Each community chooses where its data lives: Switzerland or Austria (EU). We arrange visits to the data centre for community admins and backers. Not a promise, a tour.

Rights that travel with you

Your passport does not matter

We are a European company. The GDPR applies to a company established in the EU regardless of where its users live, so anyone we serve, from Texas to Lagos, can ask for their data, take it along, or have it erased, within the GDPR's own exceptions. No US entity in our group holds or can access your data, so lawful access goes through EU law and EU courts, not through the US CLOUD Act.

Source: EDPB Guidelines 3/2018 on the territorial scope of the GDPR, section 1. This page is a plain-language summary, not legal advice, and an AI formulation of Jakob's thoughts.

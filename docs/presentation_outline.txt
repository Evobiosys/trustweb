PART 1: SEE IT (live query)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Title: "TrustInTheWeb: ask the people you trust, one hop further"
   Line: TrustWeb, by EvoBioSys, Vienna. A first-person protocol for finding people and things.
   Note: Start on this slide for ten seconds only, then go straight to the demo. Say who you are in one sentence and who you want to hear from after.

2. LIVE: ask your web
   Line: Two phones side by side, and the room is invited to join in. One person asks, "Does anyone have a motorbike I could borrow?"
   Note: The audience scans a QR code or opens the link and takes a place in the demo web with imaginary friends; the question travels and the answer returns with its path. If the network or the venue Wi-Fi fails, switch to the recorded walkthrough (slide 3).
   Fallback: the scripted three-person walkthrough in the demo ("See it in action").

3. What just happened (the second hop)
   Line: The question reached people who consented; a refusal looks exactly like "nothing found"; the answer showed it was found "through Jonas", in a friend's inventory, not mine.
   Note: Say plainly: today the live phones do one hop; this second-hop picture is the scripted version of what we are building. Honesty first, because trust is the subject.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2: WHY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. The problem: platforms versus people we trust
   Line: We want leads from friends, not strangers; a superconnector reaches only the people they know, and reposting rarely travels. [whitepaper 1.1]
   Note: Cite the weak-ties experiment (Rajkumar et al., Science, 2022 [A]) and the structural-virality study (Goel et al., Management Science, 2016 [A]); do not use the "under 4 percent" figure until it has a source [source needed].

5. Where a platform is the wrong tool
   Line: Leftover food tonight, a house-sitter, a sublet your landlord must not hear about, a bike to lend, a flat that never gets listed. [whitepaper 1.2]
   Note: Pick three of the eight cases; let the audience shout a fourth. Each is an ask that fails when broadcast.

6. We have always trusted our friends
   Line: Chimpanzees trust bonded friends more; bonobos reach out to strangers; we build on both. [whitepaper 2.5, anchor #bonobos]
   Note: Two sentences, then the honest limits (small samples, mostly young sanctuary animals, an analogy and not a proof). Cite Engelmann and Herrmann 2016, Tan and Hare 2013, Tan, Ariely and Hare 2017 [A].

7. Our philosophy in four lines
   Line: Trust we already carry; reach cannot be generated, only met; the question travels and the data stays; willingness to be asked is what we protect. [whitepaper 2.1, 2.2]
   Note: This is the first-person protocol slide: the same core, several formats (Signal Orbit). Read the four lines aloud and stop; take questions only at the end.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3: HOW
----------------------------------------------------------------------
8. The mechanism in one picture
   Line: Rings (close friend, friend, acquaintance), inventory lines with their own audience, consent at each hop, an answer that shows its path. [whitepaper 3.1 to 3.4]
   Note: Rings are one of a hundred thousand possible views, the point is the action built on top (Jakob's own framing). No score, ever.

9. Local first, no server decides
   Line: Notes and inventory stay on your phone; questions go phone to phone through a relay in Switzerland that sees routing, not content. [whitepaper 3.5]
   Note: Say what we do not claim: not a zero-knowledge system. Two places read content today, and the FAQ names them.

10. How the inventory fills
   Line: One conversation at a time (record a meeting or a spoken debrief) or one import of notes you already keep; every line remembers its source. [whitepaper 3.2]
   Note: Recording serves the querying, not the other way round. Gatherings come first because new trust forms there.

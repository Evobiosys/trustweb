TITLE
======================================================================
TrustInTheWeb: asking the people you trust, one hop further
Working subtitle: A first-person protocol for finding people and things through people you already trust
(Product name in prose: TrustWeb. Site name where the site names itself: TrustInTheWeb.org. Written by Jakob Possert-Bienzle, EvoBioSys, Vienna.)

======================================================================
ABSTRACT (one paragraph, about 120 words)
======================================================================
When we look for funding, a flat, a doctor or an introduction, we would rather ask people we trust than a platform. Acquaintances are a large source of new
opportunities, but a person can only connect people they personally know, and reposting into a group chat reaches few. TrustWeb is an infrastructure that lets a
question travel through the rings of people you trust, one consent at a time, so the answer comes back with the path it took. Each person keeps their notes and
their inventory on their own device, chooses per line who may ask, and a decline looks exactly like "nothing found". Today a question reaches a person's direct
contacts; the next hop is designed and shown in a scripted demo. This paper states the problem, the philosophy behind it, the mechanism, what exists, and the plan.

1. THE PROBLEM  (lead with the problem, then the philosophy)
======================================================================
1.1 Finding people and things through people you trust
  - We look for a job, a grant, a flat, a cleaner, a doctor, a loan of a cargo bike; we would rather ask friends for leads than strangers. [C: own site copy, idea2.site/trustweb, 2026-10-07]
  - Acquaintances are a major source of new opportunities, but recalling "who would fit" is memory work with little follow-through, because nobody is paid to do it. [A: Rajkumar et al., Science, 2022-09-15 (job mobility on LinkedIn, weak ties help, with an inverted-U shape and industry differences); the "no one is paid to do it" half is Jakob's own argument [C]]
  - A superconnector can only connect people they know. Reposting is a poor channel. [A: Goel, Anderson, Hofman, Watts, Management Science 62(1), 2016: only about 1 in 3,000 shared items grew into a large cascade.] The live site says "under 4% of matches": [source needed] (Goel et al. does not state that figure; find its origin or drop it).
  - Net effect: poor throughput of real information between people who would gladly help each other.
1.2 Where a platform is the wrong tool (strongest first, from the site's "specifically useful" list, [C])
  - Only locally relevant (leftover food tonight); trust is the main issue (house-sitting, care work); not publicly possible (subletting without the landlord knowing); only certain rings should see it;
    below the bar for a public listing (a bike, a spare room); honesty about flaws (a used car); fair pricing between people who trust each other.
1.3 Trust versus platforms
  - Platforms broadcast and rank; trust networks route and consent. A no on a platform is visible; in TrustWeb a decline is indistinguishable from no match, so refusing stays socially free. [B: repo DECISIONS.md D22, D27, D28; README Privacy Honesty Box]
  - Willingness to be asked is the scarce resource; every design rule protects it. (Jakob, signal-orbit nucleus 2026-09-14 [C]; see 2.2)
1.4 "We lead with the querying, not the recording" (Jakob 10-07 11:44 [C]): recording and notes are how the inventory fills; querying is what is new.
  [Anomaly for the site agent, not for this paper: the live "How it works" section says "Taking notes comes first". Jakob's 10-07 rule is the reverse. Not edited here.]

2. THE PHILOSOPHICAL FRAMEWORK  (Jakob 10-07: "our philosophical framework ... should go into the white paper")
======================================================================
Frame: his terms, "first person protocol", with one core and several formats (Signal Orbit: a core text plus orbits for each audience; this paper is the mini white paper drawn from the core and from the applications; Jakob 10-07 08:47 [C]).
2.1 Trust we already carry
  - His words: "The social contract and trust contract our hearts already carry": the system scales it, lets it work without our brains, and gives each other access beyond what we can convey in words quickly. [C: signal-orbit nucleus v0.3, 2026-09-14]
  - Site line: "We have always trusted our friends, and now we have a way to represent it digitally." [C: idea2.site/trustweb, 2026-10-07]. The word "always" links to 2.5.
  - First person: each person's device holds their own record; "the owner's device is the truth"; no server decides. [C: nucleus essential 8; B: repo docs/30-architecture-decisions.md]
2.2 What this protects
  - "Trust grows with use; what depletes is willingness to be asked. Every design rule protects that willingness." [C: nucleus]
  - "Reach cannot be generated, only met." Bots cannot join, because joining costs a meeting. [C: nucleus, ruling 2026-09-14]
  - The question travels, the data stays: consent chain, not transitive trust computation; a friend of a friend is not automatically a friend. [C: nucleus essential 4]
  - Paths and provenance, no scalar trust score, ever; three rings (close friend, friend, acquaintance), never a continuous number. [C: nucleus essential 6; site "discreet and discrete"]
2.3 Trust is not static, and not symmetric  (from Jakob's own TrustWeb "concepts" notes, Tana w7fWTRC7ZWds, 2026 [C])
  - Concepts he lists: hops; strength of connection; "trustworthiness is not static" ("if it's a friend I also want to show up more trustworthily").
  - "Three [strength of connection] links/[hops] to a person can be stronger than two weak ones." Candidate design principle for later multi-hop weighting. [to confirm: his notes, no source beyond himself]
  - Zutrauen and Vertrauen are listed as a concept pair (trust in someone's ability versus trust in their good faith) but the Tana nodes hold no text, and the signal-orbit file marks the moral-versus-competence distinction INTERNAL ONLY. ❗ Jakob decides whether it enters a public paper. [to confirm]
2.4 Why this can win (Tesla hypothesis, [C: nucleus, Jakob 2026-08-26])
  - It must outcompete the platforms on something they cannot provide (asking without social cost; resources reachable along trust), not merely be ethical and private.
  - "In terms of what people query, they query their friends, not other people." (Jakob, Tana BI_4kdn-iTit, 2026-09-24 [C]) : the first product bet. Hypothesis, not finding: [source needed] (no user data yet).
  - The decline that looks like "no match" is called "receding as grace" in his turquoise orbit: nobody is wounded by a refusal. [C: nucleus orbit T]
2.5 Our closest relatives (anchor #bonobos on the website: whitepaper.html#bonobos)
  - What the sources say, plainly (text for the website in the last part of this file):
    - Chimpanzees trust bonded friends more than non-friends in a non-verbal trust game: Engelmann and Herrmann, Current Biology, 2016 [A]. 15 chimpanzees, one sanctuary.
    - Wild baboon females with stronger, more stable social bonds live longer: Silk et al., Current Biology, 2010 [A]; bonds raise infant survival: Silk, Alberts, Altmann, Science, 2003 [A].
    - Bonobos: sanctuary-living bonobos shared food with strangers and, in one test, nine of fourteen chose the stranger first: Tan and Hare, PLoS ONE, 2013 [A for the paper; the "nine of fourteen" figure came from a university press summary, Duke Today, 2013-01 [B], verify in the paper]. Follow-up: young bonobos helped unfamiliar non-group members get food without being asked, plus contagious yawning toward stranger videos: Tan, Ariely, Hare, Scientific Reports 7, 2017 [A].
    - Bonobo cooperation when food is monopolisable: Hare et al., Current Biology, 2007 [A]; a 2023 conceptual replication (Nolte, Sterck, van Leeuwen, Royal Society Open Science) found the cooperation result replicated but the tolerance (co-feeding) result did not [A, read from a search summary only; full text not fetched].
    - Wider mammals: friendships lasting years in horses, elephants, hyenas, dolphins, monkeys, chimpanzees: Seyfarth and Cheney, Annual Review of Psychology, 2012 [A]; vampire bats: Carter and Wilkinson, Proc. R. Soc. B, 2013 [A]. Described as convergent evolution, not one inherited trait.
  - How it grounds "we have always trusted our friends": the chimpanzee and baboon results show selective, durable trust toward bonded partners in our nearest relatives (confidence 0.9 for "older than our species"; Jakob's earlier research note puts it at 0.95). The bonobo results add that bonds can also reach outward to unfamiliar individuals, which supports the weak-tie, one-hop-further thesis.
  - Honest limits: small samples, mostly sanctuary-living juveniles, no trust measure in the bonobo studies, no claim about wild populations or about humans; the human claim is an inference by analogy, not a result. Do not write "bonobos trust their friends": the direct trust-game evidence is chimpanzee.
  - ❗ Jakob's lines ("the 'always' ... bonobos") name bonobos, but his own earlier research (research_friendship-deep-time_2026-09-26, Tana j7YaUjve2RVb) rests on chimpanzees, baboons and bats. See report. The section is written to cover both.

3. THE MECHANISM: QUERYING FIRST
======================================================================
3.1 The ask
  - Plain words, "call into your web". The question goes to the people you are connected with, according to the ring each sits in. Each holder decides, live or in advance for a line they marked that way. [B: README, repo DECISIONS.md D22; site "Ask your web"]
  - Multi-degree asks: a question travels one hop further through a trusted friend, with consent at each hop. DESIGNED AND SCRIPTED IN THE DEMO. LIVE TODAY: direct contacts only. [B: site FAQ; screenshot w2-07 states "scripted ... the live phones do one hop today"; repo FUTURE.md lists "Multi-hop beyond one relay" as not built]
  - The answer returns with its path ("through Jonas"; "not in his inventory, in hers"), and an introduction can follow. [B: demo walkthrough]
3.2 Inventory
  - Short lines: what you could offer, lend, point to. Each line carries its own audience (only me, acquaintances, friends, close friends). Imported lines start as "only me" and are marked unreviewed; three provenance states: unreviewed, mass-imported, created step by step. [B: site FAQ]
  - Inventory fills one conversation at a time (a recorded meeting or a spoken debrief) or in one set-up import of existing notes. [B: site FAQ]
3.3 Rings and tiers
  - close friend, friend, acquaintance, chosen by the person; the ring sets what a contact may ask about. Optional (Advanced settings): restrict asks to direct contacts only. [Jakob 10-07 11:44 [C]; app status: check wave 3 app report]
3.4 Consent and the indistinguishable No
  - A decline, a block, a below-threshold result and no match look the same to the asker. [B: README Privacy Honesty Box, DECISIONS.md D22, D25 to D28]
  - Not claimed: zero-knowledge. The README states that "claiming zero-knowledge for v0 would be false"; the v1 target is real ZK properties. [B: README]
  - Two places read content today (demo inventory contact; server transcription once switched on). [B: site FAQ]
3.5 Local-first, no server decides
  - Notes, inventory, transcripts and recordings stay on the phone. Questions go phone to phone through a relay on a server in Switzerland that sees routing metadata, not content (end-to-end encrypted after the first connect step). Backups and hosted recordings are encrypted on the phone first. [B: site FAQ and privacy page; README]
  - Habit by pull, not push: you open the app because you are looking for something; push is not relied on. [C: Jakob 10-07; B: site FAQ]
3.6 Ways in and the first use case
  - Gatherings first: new trust forms there; record a meeting or a debrief; an event ring. First pilot: EA groups (Manifund application text, 2026-09-25 [C]).
  - Connecting: QR (met in person) and a link (not met in person) today; moving QR "coming soon"; NFC, Bluetooth, AirDrop "coming later". [C: Jakob 10-07 08:47 and 08:54]

11. WEBSITE TEXT FOR whitepaper.html#bonobos  (150 words; heading: "Our closest relatives: chimpanzees and bonobos")
======================================================================
We have always trusted our friends, and our closest relatives suggest how old that is. In a rope version of the human trust game, chimpanzees trusted bonded friends significantly more often than non-friends (Engelmann and Herrmann, Current Biology, 2016) [A]. Bonobos show the other half: in sanctuary experiments they shared food with unfamiliar bonobos and gave up their own food for contact with one (Tan and Hare, PLoS ONE, 2013) [A], and they helped unfamiliar non-group members without being asked (Tan, Ariely and Hare, Scientific Reports, 2017) [A]. Read together, trust that begins with friends can also reach outward through new bonds, which is what TrustWeb tries to carry one hop further. What we do not know: these were small samples, mostly young animals at one sanctuary, bonobo trust was not measured as such, and nothing here proves how humans behave. We treat it as a hypothesis worth building on.
Sources for the footnote: Engelmann and Herrmann, Cell Press, Current Biology 26(2), 2016-01-14; Tan and Hare, PLoS ONE 8(1), January 2013; Tan, Ariely and Hare, Scientific Reports 7, 2017-11-07.
